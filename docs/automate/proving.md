# Proving

Rehearsal says *it worked*. Proof says *here is the file where it says it worked, signed by math.*

The difference matters when your routine is up for promotion and someone reasonably asks: "sez who?"

---

## What counts as proof

| Evidence | Where it lives | Who verifies it |
|---|---|---|
| Sim run outcomes | `_audit` events | The kernel. Tamper = boot refusal. |
| Latency envelope (p50/p95) | `routine stats` | The promotion gate. p95 > 70% ceiling = denied. |
| Success rate | `routine stats` | The promotion gate. Sustained <70% = auto-demote. |
| Schema/policy/budget violations | Rehearsal output | Zero required. Zero exceptions. |
| Provider contract replays | Training wheels (calls 1–3) | Auto-graduation on call 4. |

All of it is generated by *execution*, not by assertion. You cannot write "97.8% success" into a form and get a rung. The number has to have happened, in the ledger, hash-chained to the runs that produced it.

## Reading the evidence

```bash
$ capcli routine stats order_refund --deep
```

```text
[dev:tier_1]  cap://order_refund@7

  trust:       reviewed
  runs:        89 total · 87 sim · 2 dev
  success:     97.8% (87/89)
  p50 / p95:   410ms / 1100ms
  envelope:    6 ops · 20s · 500 result tokens
  violations:  0 schema · 0 policy · 0 budget (last 30d)
  ops_used:    avg 4.1 / 6 declared
  provenance:  promotion evidence at op_7a1b
```

This is the résumé, and unlike most résumés, every line cites a ledger row. `87 sim runs` isn't a claim — it's 87 events you can `sys audit trace` individually.

---

## Proof survives the moment

The evidence chain is the whole reason the audit spine is append-only and hash-linked. A promotion decision cites `op_7a1b`. Six months later, when the routine has an incident and the post-mortem asks "who promoted this and on what basis?" — the answer is a walk through the DAG, not a Slack scroll of shame.

You can't retro-edit the justification. The justification *is* the history. That's the difference between governance and a diary.

---

## Proving ≠ testing (the bouncer's distinction)

- **Tests** assert what the author *thought* about. Your agent writes its own tests, which is a fox auditing a henhouse with a clipboard.
- **Proof** records what the kernel *observed*: every denial, every budget touch, every row, every wall the routine bumped into — including walls the author didn't know existed.

A suite can pass while the routine is a disaster at the ops level (slow, greedy, quota-hungry). The rehearsal envelope catches exactly those. Physics doesn't grade on participation.
